## A sibling script, not an extension of the pipeline seeder

`scripts/seed-pipeline-fixtures.ts` is 523 lines and is tuned to Phase 33's walkthrough: its
`--remove` path, its `FIXTURE_PREFIX` idempotency keys, and its refusal to delete a fixture
relationship that has gained a hand-added contact are all built around the rows it owns. Growing it
to also own registry states, timelines and follow-up dates would make one script the arbiter of two
walkthroughs, and a `--remove` for Phase 34 would then risk Phase 33's rows.

**Chosen: a sibling, `scripts/seed-fiche-fixtures.ts`, with its own `FIXTURE_PREFIX`
(`seed-fiche-`) and its own row set.** It copies the sibling's structure verbatim — the
`FORBIDDEN_ENDPOINTS` refusal, `--dry-run`, `--remove`, idempotent inserts keyed on exact SIRENs,
and the refusal to cascade-delete a fixture relationship that has gained real hand-added children.
Both seeders can be run together; neither touches the other's rows.

## The existing fixture SIRENs are FAKE, and that is load-bearing

`seed-pipeline-fixtures.ts` seeds `823456781` … `823456789`. Those are not real SIREN numbers and
the public registry will not resolve them. That makes them perfect for the `not_found` path — and
useless for proving a successful sync.

So this seeder needs **real, public, currently-active SIRENs**, and the executor must verify them
rather than take them from memory or from this plan. The verification is one command per candidate:

```
curl -s 'https://recherche-entreprises.api.gouv.fr/search?q=<SIREN>&per_page=1' | head -c 400
```

Accept a candidate only when `results[0].siren` equals the SIREN queried (which is D-05's assertion,
performed by hand). Pick large, obviously-public French companies so no customer data is implied,
and record the chosen numbers and the company names they resolved to in the SUMMARY. If the API is
unreachable at seed time, STOP and report — seeding an unverified SIREN would produce a fixture that
silently exercises the wrong branch.

## Every step names its row

The failure this plan exists to prevent is a step marked "pass" against a row that could not have
shown the state. Each step in `<how-to-verify>` therefore names its fixture row, and the SUMMARY
records the pass/fail **beside the row name**. A step whose row is missing is a FAIL, not a skip.
</decision_record>
